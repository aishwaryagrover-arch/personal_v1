IDinsight

Where the current APAR loses time and value

Limitations run across the form, the process rules and the digital system — the recommendations address all three.

01 Form design

  • Six separate cadre-wise forms10–12 pages each, with overlapping content and no common core.
  • Long and repetitiveDetails the state already holds are re-entered, alongside several open-ended narratives.
  • Overlapping grading dimensions17–25 similar attributes rated separately, adding effort but not information.
  • No descriptors, benchmark or evidence standardDimensions are never defined, no grade is set as adequate, and adverse grades need no proof.
  • Fields that do not inform performanceState of health, assets and liabilities, property return dates.
The form takes long to complete, and the grades do not discriminate.

02 Process

  • Reporting chain established late and informallyReconstructed at appraisal time, so disputes surface at the end of the cycle.
  • Duplicated assessment across stagesReviewing authorities re-grade dimensions, and Group A carries an extra Collector stage.
  • No consequence for delay, no fallback for a missing stageA missed deadline carries nothing; a vacant reporting post stalls the APAR.
  • Concerns surface only at year-endNothing is on record during the year, so an adverse grade is hard to sustain.
  • Monitoring roles not clearly definedCustodian, CMA, SDMA and state responsibilities are not consistently set out in the GR.
Nobody is accountable for moving the APAR, and nobody can see where it is.

03 Digital interface

  • Limited pre-fill, and onboarding frictionDetails, leave and training are keyed in every cycle; NIC email IDs slow onboarding.
  • No system of record for the reporting chaineHRMS carries no complete organogram, so mapping cannot be auto-populated.
  • Manual computation where systems can helpOverall grade, timeliness and workflow status are worked out by hand.
  • Workflow automation not fully utilisedReminders, escalation, auto-forwarding, disclosure and representation need manual action.
  • No dashboards and no cycle-level MISMonitoring layers cannot see pendency; grade and timeliness patterns cannot be produced.
Every rule the process relies on has to be enforced by a person.

Every area of limitation has a matching set of recommendations

Twenty-one recommendations. Fifteen follow a precedent in AIS guidance or another state; six are new for Maharashtra.

Redesigning forms

6
recommendations

Six proformas become one short Form A and one Form B, with a module for technical cadres.

  • 17–25 rated items → 7 dimensions with role-wise descriptors
  • A benchmark of 6, with extreme grades justified
  • Free-text questions cut from 12 to 4
  • Fields that do not inform performance removed
5 of 6 follow a state precedent

Streamlining processes

8
recommendations

Each stage gets an owner, a deadline and a fallback, so the cycle moves without being chased.

  • Officers map their own authorities; custodian validates
  • Auto-forward a missed stage, and record the delay
  • Reviewing officer addresses the overall grade only
  • Memos through the year; monitoring layers defined
7 of 8 follow a state precedent

Integrating data systems

7
recommendations

MahaPAR draws on records the state already holds, and does the work the form now asks people to do.

  • Pre-fill from Sevaarth, eHRMS and iGOT
  • MahaPAR calculates the overall grade and timeliness
  • Pendency dashboards for every monitoring layer
  • Disclosure and representation handled in the portal
3 of 7 follow a state precedent
