IDinsight

Where the current APAR loses time and value

Limitations run across three areas — the form, the process rules and the digital system. The recommendations address all three together.

01Form design

  • Six separate cadre-wise forms10–12 pages each across six variants, with large sections of overlapping content and no common core.
  • Long and repetitiveOfficer details, posting, leave and training history are re-entered every year although the state already holds them.
  • Overlapping grading dimensions17–25 similar attributes are rated separately, adding effort without adding information.
  • No descriptors, benchmark or evidence standardDimensions are named but never defined, nothing states what grade is adequate, and no documentation is required for an adverse grade.
  • High qualitative narrative burdenSeveral open-ended questions ask officers to describe work, difficulties, training needs and overall assessment.
  • Fields that do not inform performanceState of health, assets and liabilities, property return dates.
The form takes long to complete and the grades do not discriminate.

02Process

  • Reporting chain established late and informallyWho reported on whom is reconstructed at appraisal time; disputes surface at the end of the cycle.
  • Duplicated assessment across stagesReviewing authorities re-grade dimensions and give detailed inputs even where they agree.
  • An extra approval layer for Group AThe Collector / Divisional Commissioner stage adds time without adding assessment.
  • No consequence for delay, no fallback for a missing stageA missed deadline carries nothing, and a vacant reporting post stalls the APAR indefinitely.
  • Concerns surface only at year-endNothing is on record during the year, so an adverse grade is hard to sustain and easy to contest.
  • Monitoring roles not clearly definedCustodian, CMA, SDMA and state responsibilities are not consistently set out in the GR.
Nobody is accountable for moving the APAR, and nobody can see where it is.

03Digital interface

  • Onboarding frictionDependency on NIC email IDs slows getting officials onto the portal.
  • Limited pre-fill from other state systemsOfficer details, posting, leave and completed iGOT training are entered manually every cycle.
  • No system of record for the reporting chaineHRMS carries no complete organogram, so authority mapping cannot be auto-populated.
  • Manual computation where systems can helpOverall grade, timeliness indicators and workflow status are computed or tracked manually.
  • Workflow automation not fully utilisedReminders, escalation, auto-forwarding, disclosure and representation need manual intervention.
  • No role-specific monitoring dashboardsCustodians and monitoring authorities lack views of pending, completed and overdue APARs by stage.
  • No cycle-level management informationGrade distribution, timeliness patterns and cadre-wise comparison cannot be produced.
Every rule the process relies on has to be enforced by a person.

Redesigning forms 6

  • One Form A (Group A and B) and one Form B (Group C) replace six proformas, with a technical cadre moduleOdisha · Nagaland
  • 17–25 rated items → 7 dimensions at 40/30/30, with role-wise descriptors in Annexure 3Odisha · Rajasthan · Gujarat
  • A benchmark of 6; extreme and below-benchmark grades must be justifiedNagaland · Gujarat
  • Non-performance fields removed: health, assets, property returnsOdisha · Nagaland · Manipur
  • Free-text questions cut from 12 to 4 across all stages, with set word limitsAIS · Goa · Gujarat
  • Instructions and examples moved to an annexure so the form holds its page limitIDinsight

  • Officers map their own reporting and reviewing authority; the custodian validatesGAD feedback
  • NRC auto-generated where tenure is 3 months or less, or no authority was in positionOdisha

  • Accepting authority acts only where a stage is missingGujarat · Odisha
  • Auto-forward a stage when its deadline passes, and record the delayAIS · Gujarat · Odisha
  • Memos issued through the year, with the officer's response on recordOdisha
  • Fixed closure date; online filing only, signed with DSC or eSignAIS · Odisha · Nagaland
  • Monitoring layers defined: custodian, CMA, SDMA, GADGujarat
