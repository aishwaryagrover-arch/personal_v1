MahaPAR user windows: filling the APAR at each stage

Form A (Group A and B) and Form B (Group C) translated into the screens an officer, a reporting officer, a reviewing officer, an establishment officer and an accepting authority actually see. Layout, colour and components follow the eHRMS app APAR windows: grey app bar with a centred title, white cards on a light background, the blue name block, the step timeline with green ticks and an amber hourglass, and blue "View" pills. These are work windows, not dashboards. Every field maps to a numbered item in the form drafts.

The officer opens the APAR from Track My PAR. Part 1 arrives pre-filled from Sevaarth; the officer only confirms the reporting chain and leave. Part 2 is split one question per screen so the word limits stay visible and nothing scrolls out of sight.

Design decisions carried over from the eHRMS APAR windows

  • Same shell. Grey status and app bar, back chevron on the left, navy title centred, content as white rounded cards on a light grey body.
  • The blue name block identifies whose APAR is open on every screen of every role — the single thing an officer with 24 pending APARs needs at a glance.
  • The step timeline from Track My PAR is kept as-is: green tick for done, amber hourglass for the live stage, grey for what is ahead, with the auto-forward warning in the amber banner the app already uses.
  • Blue pill actions ("View", "Open") and the blue primary button; saffron only for a deadline or representation action, red only for a breach.

Decisions the forms forced, which the eHRMS screens do not cover

  • One question per screen for Part 2, so a 100-word limit and its counter stay on screen together. Word counters are live and block submission over the limit.
  • Conditional fields open, they do not merely appear. A grade of 1–2 or 9–10 unlocks the justification in 3.3/3.4; an overall grade below 6 opens the memo attachment; "Fit" with doubtful integrity blocks the signature until a reason is written.
  • System data sits on the dimension it informs as a small grey chip, never as a score. This keeps the form's rule — reference, not a separate weight — visible at the moment of grading.
  • The reviewing officer's agree path is two taps. Revision opens per dimension, and the recalculated overall grade shows the old value beside the new one.
  • Every stage ends in one DSC or eSign action that locks the part and names where it goes next. Nothing in these windows can be printed and signed on paper.

Open, pending GAD decisions

  • Scoring weights in Part 3.1 are shown as drafted (40/30/30 for Form A, 50/50 for Form B); the screens read them from configuration.
  • The split of timeliness marks between items is not set, so the timeliness screen shows the total only.
  • The technical-cadre module (2.6) is a branch point only; content is to be designed with each department.
  • Marathi is the first language of these screens. The English shown here is for review; field labels will need Marathi lengths checked, since several run longer than the card width.
