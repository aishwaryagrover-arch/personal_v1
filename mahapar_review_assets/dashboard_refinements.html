Suggested refinements to the APAR dashboards developed by NIC

Nineteen observations from a walkthrough of the SPARROW APAR screens, each framed as a low-effort refinement that works within the existing design, and each referenced to the screen on which it was observed.

Within the present design

Every suggestion keeps NIC's existing layout, colour scheme and components. Nothing here calls for a new screen, a new report or a change to the underlying data model. Where a suggestion needs more than a change of wording or order, this is stated plainly.

Referenced to the screen

Each item carries the screen on which the point was observed, so that NIC can locate it directly. Click any screenshot to enlarge it. Beside it is the suggested form of the same element, drawn in NIC's own visual style.

Effort is our reading, not an estimate

The effort noted against each suggestion is IDinsight's assessment from the screens alone. NIC is best placed to confirm it, and we would welcome correction where our reading of the effort involved is mistaken.

A note on sequencing. Suggestions 17 and 2 — the spelling corrections and the wording of the performance labels — cost very little and are the points most readily noticed when the system is demonstrated. They may be worth taking up first. Suggestions 4, 9 and 16 would, in our assessment, make the greatest practical difference to the monitoring authorities, and would merit discussion with NIC on sequencing.

The suggestions in detail

One entry per observation, in group order.

1

Align the status label with the threshold scale already defined in the filter

Group A
As presently displayed in SPARROW
Click to enlarge
What we observed
The filter on the Instance Dashboard defines four bands: Excellent 95%+, Good 80–94.9%, Warning 60–79.9%, Poor below 60%. The service cards presently display the label GOOD at values between 60% and 77%, which fall in the Warning band by that same scale.
Suggested refinement
Drive the label from the thresholds already written into the filter, so a service at 66.88% is shown as Warning and the card and the filter tell the same story. No new calculation is required; the bands exist.
EffortVery low — a single mapping between the existing bands and the label.
Indicative form of the suggestion
MAHA-MLRWARNING
66.88%
616 Officers | 412 Generated | 14 Closed
Warning band 60–79.9%
Why it helps. A senior officer scanning 70 cards reads the label before the number. Aligning the two means the dashboard can be relied upon at a glance, which is what it is for.

3

State the DPC eligibility threshold that the report actually applies

Group A
As presently displayed in SPARROW
Click to enlarge
What we observed
The note below the DPC report reads “Employees with a total grade ≥ 30 are marked eligible.” The Total Grade column is out of 30, and officers are being marked eligible at 27.52 and 27.31. The stated rule and the applied rule appear to differ.
Suggested refinement
Restate the note to match the rule in use — for example “Officers with a total grade of 27 or above across the three years are marked eligible.” If the intended rule is an average rather than a total, say so in the same line.
EffortVery low — one line of text, once the intended rule is confirmed with GAD.
Indicative form of the suggestion

Anand Ashok Katake9.009.229.3027.52Yes
Note: Officers with a total grade of 27 or above across the three assessment years are marked eligible. Hover over a grade to see which preceding years were used.
Why it helps. The DPC report supports promotion decisions and may be placed before a committee. A note that matches the computation protects the report from question at that stage.

Bring the pendency columns to the front of the Service Dashboard

Group A
As presently displayed in SPARROW
Click to enlarge
What we observed
The Service Dashboard carries seven columns recording where an APAR is presently pending — with the custodian, the officer, the reporting authority, the reviewing authority, the accepting authority and the CR section. These sit to the right of the table and are reached only by horizontal scrolling, and several hold a value of zero for every row.
Suggested refinement
Place the pendency columns immediately after the organisation name, move the APAR count columns to the right, and suppress any column holding zero for every row in the selected year. A “Pending with” group heading can span them.
EffortLow — column order and a rule to hide all-zero columns. No change to the query.

Group A
As presently displayed in SPARROW

What we observed
The summary tile reports 616 total officers while the table total below reports 611. Separately, APARs generated (868) exceeds officers posted (611), which is consistent with officers having more than one reporting period in the year, though this is not stated on screen.

What we observed
Employee Search is a mandatory field on the Officers APAR Pending Report and the Officer Pen Picture Report. A user must therefore know an officer's name before the report will run, and the result returns one officer at a time.

Avoid defaulting the same officer to both authorities, and stagger the two target dates

Group E
As presently displayed in SPARROW
Click to enlarge
What we observed
On the Channel of Submission step, the same officer appears selected as both Reporting Authority and Reviewing Authority, and both target dates default to the same date, 31/10/2026. Separately, Form Type on the preceding step is a required field presented without an indication of which form applies to which group of officers.
Suggested refinement
Leave the reviewing authority blank for the custodian to select, and display a confirmation message where the same officer is chosen for both roles. Default the reviewing target date after the reporting target date. Against Form Type, show the applicable form for the officer's group, for example “Form A — Group A and B officers”.
EffortLow — default values, one confirmation message, and a help line against Form Type.
Indicative form of the suggestion
STAGENAMETARGET DATE
Reporting AuthorityShri Vaseem Anwar Sayyad31/07/2026
Reviewing AuthoritySelect reviewing authority30/09/2026
The reporting and reviewing authority are the same officer. Please confirm this is intended before proceeding.
Why it helps. The two-stage check is the core safeguard in the APAR process. A default that places one officer in both roles may be carried forward unnoticed when workflows are created in bulk.

Features we would suggest retaining

Noted explicitly so that they are not altered in the course of taking up the suggestions above.

Drill-through from the aggregate to named officers

From the regional figure to the list of officers whose workflow has not been created, with Excel and CSV export. This is the step that makes the dashboard actionable, and it already works.

The four-step workflow creation wizard

Numbered progress, bulk selection, and Not Started / In Process / Completed filters on the officer list. The structure is sound and should be retained as the pattern for other multi-step screens.

The DPC report's method note and hover facility

The report states how the weighted grade is calculated and allows the user to hover to see which preceding years were used. This degree of openness about method is unusual and valuable, particularly for a report placed before a committee.

Suggested next steps

Offered for GAD's consideration in the first instance, and thereafter for discussion with NIC.

  1. GAD's view on the list. Confirm which suggestions GAD wishes to take up, and whether any further points should be added from the department's own experience of the system.
  2. Confirm the two rules that need a departmental decision. Suggestion 3 requires GAD to confirm the DPC eligibility threshold the report should apply. Suggestion 14 requires the refresh interval beyond which dashboard figures should be flagged as dated.
  3. Discussion with NIC on effort and sequence. NIC to confirm or correct the indicative effort against each suggestion, and to advise which can be taken up in the current release.
  4. A short first set. We would suggest the wording and ordering changes be taken up together as a first set, as they require no change to data or queries.
  5. Review after the first set is deployed. A walkthrough with GAD and NIC once the first set is live, before taking a view on the remainder.

Two points for GAD's decision

DPC eligibility threshold. The report's note and its computation presently state different thresholds. The correct rule is a departmental matter rather than a technical one, and NIC will need it confirmed before the note can be corrected.

Refresh interval for the dashboards. Suggestion 14 proposes that figures be flagged once they exceed an agreed age. That interval is for GAD to set, having regard to how the dashboards are used in review meetings.

What this note does not cover

The observations here are confined to the dashboard and report screens recorded in October 2026. They do not extend to the APAR forms themselves, to the officer's filing experience, or to the data held in the source systems, each of which is being taken up separately.

Acknowledgement

The screens reviewed represent a substantial body of work covering officer, service, instance and departmental levels, together with workflow creation and a set of departmental reports. The suggestions here are offered in that context.
